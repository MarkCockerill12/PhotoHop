Welcome to PhotoHop

PhotoHop puts you (or anything else) into another place — a game level, a wallpaper video, a Blender render or a real photo — and makes it look like you were really there. It is a small photo and video editor built for that one job: cut out, place, then match colour, light, shadows, grain and lens so everything reads as one picture.

The idea in one minute

A PhotoHop project is a stack of layers, like sheets of glass on top of each other. The bottom layer is the background (the place). Layers on top are the things you add (you, a prop, a character). Everything you do is non-destructive: your original files are never changed, and every step can be undone with Ctrl+Z.

For each layer you add, the inspector on the right shows numbered steps. Work down them:

  1. Cut out — keep only you, hide the rest of your photo. See Cut out.
  2. Position & size — drag yourself into place. See Position & size.
  3. Fit into the scene — one click matches colours, adds a shadow and grain. See Fit into the scene.
  4. Colour adjustments — fine-tune by hand if you want. See Colour adjustments.
  5. Shadow & light — adjust the shadow on the ground and the light direction. See Shadow & light.
  6. Camera & lens look — grain, blur, haze and glow so you look shot with the same camera. See Camera & lens look.

Then File > Export saves the finished picture or video. See Saving & exporting.

New here? Follow Your first composite — it walks through a whole picture in about five minutes.

Finding your way around this manual

  • The contents on the left are grouped from beginner to reference.
  • Search (the box at the top) finds every section that mentions all the words you type. Results show the page and section; click one to jump there.
  • Every section in the app has a ? Help button that opens the matching page here.
  • Press F1 anywhere in PhotoHop to open or close the manual.
  • Open in web browser shows the same manual as one web page you can keep, print or read on another screen. The release download also includes it as PhotoHop-Manual.html.

What PhotoHop will and won't do with AI

AI is optional. When it is used, it only makes masks (which pixels belong to you) and settings (colour and light values you can see and change). It never repaints your picture. The single exception is Remove an object, which paints only inside the object you asked to remove, on a separate layer you can hide. See AI tools.

  • File — new, open, save, import, export, and the Blender round trip. See Menus.

The toolbar

Click a tool or press its key. The right part of the toolbar shows options for the current tool (brush size, selection shape and so on). See Tools.

The Layers panel

Each row has an eye (show/hide), a lock, a small icon for the kind of layer (photo, video, paint, colour) and the layer's name. Click a name to select it. The arrows on the right move a layer up or down. The buttons at the top add layers. See Layers.

The canvas

Shows the result. Scroll to zoom towards the mouse pointer. To pan (move the view), drag with the right or middle mouse button — on a touchpad, right-click (two-finger or corner click), hold and drag — or hold Space and drag. Ctrl+0 fits the whole picture in the window.

The inspector

When a layer is selected, the inspector shows its settings as collapsible sections. For the bottom (background) layer you only see the sections that make sense for a background. With nothing selected, it shows the Canvas settings: the picture size, frame rate, length and background colour. Hover over any slider name to see what it does.

The timeline

Play/pause, step frame by frame, and see each layer as a bar in time. Diamond (◆) markers are keyframes. See Timing & animation.

Your first composite

This walk-through puts a photo of a person into a game screenshot. It takes about five minutes. You need two pictures: a background (for example a screenshot from noclip.website) and a photo of yourself (a phone photo is fine).

1. Bring in the background

Drag the background picture onto PhotoHop's window, or use File > Import photo / video (Ctrl+I). The first picture you import sets the size of the project (the canvas).

2. Bring in yourself

Import your photo the same way. It appears as a new layer on top. Select it in the Layers list (it's probably selected already).

3. Cut yourself out

Open step 1 Cut out in the inspector and click Remove background (AI). After a few seconds (up to a minute on a laptop without a graphics card) the background of your photo disappears.

Fix any mistakes: tick Paint on the cut-out, then use the Brush (B) to hide bits that should be gone and the Eraser (E) to bring back bits that went missing. See Cut out.

No AI installed? Use the Pen (P) to draw around yourself, or the Select (M) tool's lasso. See Tools.

4. Place and size yourself

Press V for the Move tool. Drag yourself to where you should stand; drag a corner handle to resize (hold Shift to stretch freely) and the top handle to rotate. Think about where your feet touch the ground and how tall you'd be at that distance — compare with doors, people or objects in the background. See Position & size.

5. Fit into the scene

Open step 3 Fit into the scene and click ✨ One-click fit. PhotoHop:

  • matches your colours to the scene around you (light colour, black level, saturation),
  • turns on a shadow on the ground, starting at your feet,
  • copies the background's film grain so you don't look too clean.

Drag Match strength to compare the original with the matched look. See Fit into the scene.

6. Fine-tune

  • Shadow pointing the wrong way? With the Move tool, drag the orange dot to where the shadow of your head should land — copy the direction of shadows already in the scene. See Shadow & light.
  • Too bright, too dark, too orange? Open 4 Colour adjustments and nudge Exposure or Temperature. See Colour adjustments.

7. Export

File > Export…, choose *PNG* or *JPEG* under Current frame as image and click Export image…. Save the project too (Ctrl+S) so you can come back to it. See Saving & exporting.

Layers

Everything in a PhotoHop project is a layer. Layers stack from bottom to top: the top of the Layers list is the front of the picture.

Kinds of layer

IconKindWhat it holds

filmVideoa video clip (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM, AVI…) or an image sequence
brushPaintan empty layer you paint on with the Brush
squareColourone flat colour (useful with a blend mode as a tint)

Adding layers

  • Import: drag files onto the window, use the picture button at the top of the Layers panel, or File > Import (Ctrl+I). You can pick several files at once.
  • New paint layer and New colour layer: the brush and square buttons at the top of the Layers panel, or the Layer menu.
  • Duplicate (Ctrl+D) copies the selected layer, including its cut-out and settings.

The first picture you import into an empty project sets the canvas size; the first video also sets the frame rate and length.

The Layers panel

  • Eye — show or hide the layer.
  • Lock — protects the layer from being moved on the canvas.
  • Name — click to select. A red name means the layer's file could not be loaded (hover to see why), for example because it was moved.
  • Arrows — move the layer up or down the stack. A layer must be above the background to be fitted into it.
  • Bin (top) — delete the selected layer (Del).

Renaming

Type a new name in the box at the top of the inspector.

The background layer

The bottom layer is treated as the background. Its inspector shows only Position & size, Colour adjustments, Camera & lens look (for depth of field and bloom on the background), Game look and Replace a window or screen. The cut-out, fit and shadow steps appear on layers above it.

Several objects in one picture

If one picture contains several separate things (three characters, two props), use Split into separate objects so each gets its own shadow and colour match. See Several objects in one picture.

Position & size

Step 2 Position & size controls where a layer sits, how big it is and how it mixes with the layers below.

On the canvas (easiest)

Choose the Move tool (V):

  • Drag inside the layer to move it.
  • Drag a corner handle to resize, keeping proportions. Hold Shift to stretch freely.
  • Drag the top handle to rotate. Hold Shift to rotate in 15° steps.

In the inspector

SettingWhat it does
Opacity0% invisible, 100% solid
Positionx and y of the layer's centre, in canvas pixels
Sizewidth and height in percent of the original
Rotationdegrees, clockwise
Blend modehow the layer mixes with what's below. Normal for almost everything
Colour(colour layers only) the fill colour

Drag a number left/right to change it, or double-click it to type.

  • Reset puts the layer back in the centre at its original size.
  • Fit to canvas scales the layer so it fills the canvas.

Blend modes

PhotoHop has 19 blend modes with the same maths as GIMP: Normal, Multiply (darkens — good for shadows and tints), Screen (lightens — good for glows), Overlay, Soft light, Hard light, Add, Subtract, Difference, Dodge, Burn, Grain extract, Grain merge, Luminance and more. Everything is mixed in linear light, so results are physically correct and there's no 8-bit banding.

Getting the size right

Real compositing tip: find something of known size near where you'll stand (a door is about 2 m, a person 1.7 m) and size yourself against it at the same distance. Your feet should be on the ground where the shadow will start — the shadow and colour match both use the bottom of your cut-out as your feet.

Animating position

Each row has a ◆ button that sets a keyframe at the playhead. See Timing & animation.

Tools

Tools live in the toolbar. Hover a tool to see its name; press its key to switch.

Move (V)

See Position & size. With a shadow turned on, an orange dot shows where the shadow of the top of the layer lands; drag it to aim the shadow. See Shadow & light.

Select an area (M)

Choose the shape in the toolbar: Rectangle, Ellipse, Lasso (drag around) or Magic wand (click a colour; Tolerance sets how similar colours must be).

  • A plain selection replaces the cut-out with what you selected.
  • Hold Shift to add to it, Alt to subtract from it.
  • Feather softens the selection's edge (in pixels).

The selection always goes into the selected layer's cut-out (its mask).

Crop the canvas (C)

Drag a rectangle on the canvas, then press Enter to crop the whole project to it (all layers keep their place). Esc cancels.

Brush (B) and Eraser (E)

Options in the toolbar: Size (pixels), Hardness (soft to hard edge), Opacity and, for the brush, the colour.

  • On a paint layer, the brush paints and the eraser erases.
  • On a layer with a cut-out and Paint on the cut-out ticked (in Cut out), the brush hides and the eraser brings back. The toolbar shows *Editing mask* in yellow while this is on.

Pen (P)

Draws bezier shapes that become part of the cut-out:

  • Click to add a point; drag while adding to pull out a smooth curve.
  • Click the first point or press Enter to close the shape.
  • Drag points and handles to adjust; Alt-drag a handle to break the curve into a corner.
  • Del removes the point under the mouse; Esc finishes.

Each shape can Add (keep inside) or Subtract (cut away), be inverted, feathered and faded. On video, press the shape's ◆ at several frames and it follows by moving between them. See Cut out.

Hand (H)

Drag to pan. With any tool you can also pan by dragging with the right or middle mouse button (touchpad: right-click, hold and drag) or holding Space while dragging. Scroll to zoom.

Cut out

Step 1 Cut out keeps the person or object and hides the rest of the layer's picture. In PhotoHop a cut-out is a mask: a black-and-white picture where white means *show* and black means *hide*. Masks are never destructive — your original picture is untouched and you can fix the mask at any time.

Remove background (AI)

The big Remove background (AI) button finds the main subject and hides everything else. On a video layer it becomes Remove background (AI, whole video) and tracks the subject through every frame.

  • Photos take a few seconds on a graphics card, up to a minute on a laptop CPU.
  • It needs the AI tools installed. See AI tools.

Keep only… (describe it)

Opens a small window where you type what to keep — "person", "dog", "toy gun", "car on the left". AI selects just that. Useful when the photo has several things and you want one of them.

Remove a colour

For plain backgrounds — a studio wall, a green screen, the flat grey behind a 3D render — remove one colour everywhere at once:

  1. Click Pick a colour to remove.
  2. Click the colour on the canvas (e.g. the background). Every pixel close to that colour disappears, anywhere in the picture — not only the area you clicked, so gaps between arms and legs go too. A small swatch shows the picked colour.
  3. Adjust Tolerance (how different a colour may be and still be removed: raise it if bits of the background remain, lower it if parts of the object vanish) and Softness (a fade for smooth edges). The change updates live.

Ctrl+Z undoes. Pick again to remove a second colour. It combines with AI cut-outs, painting and pen shapes.

Object contains the same colour as the background (a grey shirt on a grey wall)? Remove the colour, then bring the shirt back with the Eraser while *Paint on the cut-out* is ticked.

Fix it by hand

After any cut-out:

  • Cut-out on — untick to see the whole picture again (handy for checking).
  • Invert — keep the outside instead (cut a hole).
  • Paint on the cut-out — the Brush hides and the Eraser brings back. Use a soft brush (low hardness) for hair and fuzzy edges, a hard one for clothes and objects.
  • Remove the cut-out — deletes the mask so the whole picture shows again.
  • Paint the cut-out (when there is no cut-out yet) — starts an all-visible mask and switches to the brush, for cutting out completely by hand.
  • Select an area (M) — use rectangle, ellipse, lasso or magic wand selections. See Tools.
Zoom in (mouse wheel) when cleaning edges. Hide the background layer for a moment (its eye) to see leftovers against the empty canvas.

Pen shapes

For exact outlines (a phone, a gun, a window frame), draw shapes with the Pen (P). The list under Pen shapes shows each shape:

  • Click a shape's name to edit it with the pen.
  • The checkbox turns it on or off.
  • Add / Subtract: Add keeps what's inside, Subtract cuts it away (e.g. the gap between an arm and the body).
  • Invert flips that one shape.
  • ◆ keyframes the shape at the playhead (for video: key it at a few frames and it moves smoothly between them).
  • The bin deletes it.
  • While editing a shape: Feather (soft edge, pixels) and Opacity.

Pen shapes combine with the AI or painted mask.

Split into separate objects

If the picture holds several separate objects (three characters in one render), click Split into separate objects. See Several objects in one picture.

Tips for a convincing cut-out

  • Check the edges at 100% zoom. A thin line of the old background around you is the most common giveaway.
  • Hair: keep it slightly soft rather than chopping it off.
  • The bottom of the cut-out is treated as your feet for the shadow; trim anything below the feet (floor, reflection).

Fit into the scene

Step 3 Fit into the scene makes a layer look like it belongs in the background. Colour is the biggest giveaway in any composite, followed by a missing shadow and a too-clean, too-sharp look. This section fixes all three.

One-click fit

✨ One-click fit does three things in one go (one undo step each, all adjustable afterwards):

  1. Colour match — the same as *Match colours* below.
  2. Shadow — turns on a shadow on the ground starting at the layer's feet. If another layer in the project already has a shadow, it copies its direction, length and darkness (one sun for the whole scene) and scales the softness to this layer's size. Otherwise it aims the shadow away from the light it finds in the background.
  3. Grain — copies the background's film/sensor noise.

It never adds blur. If you want the background's softness or motion blur too, use Copy the camera's blur & grain in Camera & lens look.

Match colours (instant, no AI)

Measures the background around the layer and changes six ordinary colour sliders so the layer sits in that light:

What it matchesHowSlider it sets
Colour of the lightcompares only grey/white parts (a white shirt, grey metal) with grey parts of the sceneTemperature, Tint
Black levelthe darkest parts should be as dark (or as hazy) as the scene'sShadow level, Contrast
Brightnesshighlights never brighter than the scene's; a little pull towards its mid-tonesExposure, Contrast
Saturationa gentle pull towards the scene's, so very vivid colours calm downSaturation

Why your colours stay your colours

Matching only the light — not the object's own colours — is what lets PhotoHop match any colour into any scene. A deep-blue alien stays blue in a green-and-orange level; it just gets the scene's warm light, black level and saturation. If the layer has no grey or white parts at all (a fully blue figure), PhotoHop assumes it was lit with neutral light and only adds the scene's light colour.

This also removes coloured room lighting from photos of yourself: if you were lit by purple LEDs, your white or grey clothes show it, and the match replaces that purple with the scene's light.

Match with AI

Match with AI asks an AI model (Harmonizer) how the layer should look in this scene. PhotoHop then finds the six slider values that reproduce that suggestion as closely as possible — so the result is always ordinary, editable sliders, never AI pixels. It is slower than *Match colours* and needs the AI tools installed. Try both and keep the one you like (Ctrl+Z undoes).

Match strength: Original ↔ Matched

After a match, a Match strength slider appears. Drag it to slide every changed setting between your original look (0%) and the matched look (100%). Watch the sliders in 4 Colour adjustments move as you drag — they show exactly what the match changed.

  • Tweak any colour slider after matching and the strength slider keeps your tweak at every strength.
  • The line under the slider lists the changes, e.g. *Temperature +1.10, Shadow level +0.11*.
  • Forget the match keeps the current sliders but removes the strength slider.

Match to

Chooses where the colours to match come from:

  • The scene around this layer (best) — the background in a band around the layer, plus a little of the whole picture.
  • All of "<layer>" — the whole of another layer, e.g. a reference photo shot in the same place.

When the match looks wrong

  • Layer must be above the background. Use the arrows in the Layers list.
  • Leftover background in the cut-out (a grey studio wall, green screen spill) confuses the match. Clean the cut-out first.
  • Several objects in one layer standing in different places (one in sunlight, one in shade) get one compromise. Split them first: Several objects in one picture.
  • Lower Match strength if the change is too strong, or adjust single sliders.

Several objects in one picture

Sometimes one picture holds several separate things — for example a render of three characters: two in the foreground and one far away on a hill. They stand in different places, so each needs its own shadow (different feet, different size, different softness) and its own colour match (different surroundings). One layer can only have one of each, so split the picture first.

Split into separate objects

  1. Cut out the picture as usual (see Cut out) so only the objects are visible.
  2. Select the layer and click Split into separate objects in step 1 Cut out (or Layer > Split into separate objects).

PhotoHop finds every separate object (anything with a gap around it), and for each one:

  • creates a new layer named *<name> 1*, *<name> 2*, … from left to right, cropped tightly around that object and placed exactly where it was;
  • copies the original layer's colour and effect settings;
  • finds that object's feet (the lowest solid part) for its shadow.

The original layer is kept, hidden, and renamed *(original)* — show it again (eye button) if you need it. Undo (Ctrl+Z) puts everything back.

Pieces that are close together (a gap in an arm, a weapon held in a hand) stay with their object. Tiny specks are ignored.

Then fit each object

Select each new layer in turn and click ✨ One-click fit:

  • Each gets a colour match from its own surroundings — the figure on the green hill matches the hill, the ones on the platform match the platform.
  • Each gets a shadow from its own feet, with the same sun direction as the other shadows in the scene and softness scaled to its size, so a small far-away figure gets a small, tighter shadow.

Fine-tuning per object

  • Shadow direction: with the Move tool, drag each object's orange dot. Copy the direction of a real shadow in the background. All objects lit by the same sun should point the same way.
  • Far away objects usually need less contrast, a little Haze (in Camera & lens look) and softer, fainter shadows.
  • Ground that slopes (a hill): lower the shadow's Length so it doesn't climb the slope, and adjust Feet line if the shadow starts too high or low.

Doing it by hand instead

You can also Duplicate (Ctrl+D) the layer once per object and, on each copy, use the Pen or Brush to hide the other objects. Splitting is faster and keeps each layer small.

Colour adjustments

Step 4 Colour adjustments changes brightness and colour by hand. A colour match (step 3) sets these same sliders for you; you can tweak them afterwards. Every slider is keyframable on video (with *Animate* on, see Timing & animation).

The everyday sliders

SliderWhat it doesTip
Exposureoverall brightness in camera stops (+1 = twice as bright)match how bright you look vs. things next to you
Temperatureleft = cooler/bluer, right = warmer/more orangesunset = warm, shade or night = cool
Tintleft = greener, right = more magentafixes green fluorescent or pink LED casts
Contrastdifference between darks and brights (1 = unchanged)hazy or far-away scenes have less
Saturation0 = black and white, 1 = unchangedgame scenes are often less vivid than phone photos
Shadow levelraises or lowers the darkest parts (black level)match the blackest blacks in the scene

On turns all colour adjustments of this layer off and on to compare. Reset all colour goes back to the original colours (and forgets the colour match).

Colour wheels (shadows / midtones / highlights)

Three wheels tint and brighten three ranges separately. Colourists call them Lift (shadows), Gamma (midtones) and Gain (highlights).

  • Drag the dot towards a colour to tint that range. Double-click to reset.
  • The number under each wheel brightens or darkens that range.
  • Contrast centre (pivot) is the brightness that Contrast leaves unchanged.

Example: a scene with blue shadows and warm sunlight → drag the Shadows wheel slightly towards blue and the Highlights wheel slightly towards orange.

Curves

Remap brightness with a curve: the horizontal axis is the input, the vertical the output. Bend the line up to brighten that range, down to darken it. Choose Master (all channels), Red, Green or Blue to change one colour channel.

  • Click to add a point, drag to move it, double-click a point to delete it.
  • Reset curve straightens it.

Change one colour only

Selects a range of colours and changes only those (colourists call this a *qualifier*). Example: make a green shirt blue.

  1. Tick Enabled.
  2. Set Hue (which colour) and Hue width (how broad a range), Sat low/high and Lum low/high to narrow it down. Softness smooths the edge of the selection.
  3. Tick Show matte to see what's selected (white = selected). Untick when done.
  4. Change it with Hue shift, Saturation and Brightness. Invert changes everything except the selected colours.

Checking colour with scopes

Your eyes adapt; scopes don't. Press S for the Scopes to compare your layer's black level and colour with the background's.

Shadow & light

Step 5 Shadow & light gives a layer a shadow on the ground, lets shadows in the scene fall on it, and can relight it from another direction. A missing or wrong shadow is the second most common giveaway after colour.

Aim from the scene's light

Aim from the scene's light estimates where the light in the background comes from and its colour, then points the shadow away from it and sets the relight colour. It's a good start; real shadows already in the scene are a better guide (see below).

Shadow on the ground

Tick Shadow on the ground. PhotoHop finds the feet (the lowest solid part of the cut-out) and throws the layer's silhouette onto the ground, sharp near the feet and softer further away, plus a short dark contact patch where the feet touch the ground.

SettingWhat it does
Directionwhich way the shadow falls, degrees (0 = right, 90 = towards you, -90 = away)
Lengthcompared to the layer's height: low sun = long, midday = short
Thicknesswidens a shadow that looks paper-thin when the light comes from the side (a cut-out is flat)
Softnessblur of the shadow edge in pixels: sunny = low, cloudy = high
Darknesshow dark the shadow is
Contact darknessthe small dark patch right under the feet
Feet linewhere the layer touches the ground (fraction of its height, 1 = bottom edge). Find feet sets it
Shadow colourshadows outdoors are slightly blue (lit by the sky)

Aiming the shadow

The easiest way: choose the Move tool (V). An orange dot shows where the shadow of the top of the layer lands. Drag the dot to where it should be. Look at shadows already in the background — a lamp post, a tree, a crate — and match their direction and length.

All shadows from the same sun point the same way and have the same length relative to the height of what casts them. Copy a shadow that is already in the picture.

Body-shaped shadow (AI)

A cut-out photo is flat, so a light from the side throws a thin, odd shadow. Body-shaped shadow (AI) rebuilds a person in 3D from the photo (SAM 3D Body) and projects the real body shape onto the ground. Tick Use 3D body to use it. It's for people; for other objects use Thickness.

Scene shadows fall on me

If you stand where shadows fall — sun through leaves, window frames, blinds — those shadows should fall on you too. Tick Scene shadows fall on me: PhotoHop reads where the ground near you is sunlit or shaded and lays the same pattern over you, following the ground shadow's direction.

  • Line up your ground shadow with the scene's shadows first (it can be invisible: set Darkness to 0).
  • Strength — how dark the scene's shadows are on you. Softness — blur of the pattern. Sun / shade split — moves the line between sunlit and shaded ground.
  • My ground shadow only darkens sunlit ground — your shadow doesn't double-darken places that are already in shade.

Light direction on me (relight)

Changes which side of the layer is lit, so it matches the scene's sun — for people, characters and objects alike. Tick Light direction on me (relight).

Relight needs to know which way each part of the layer faces (its *shape*, stored as a normal map):

  • Shape from outline (no AI, instant) guesses a rounded shape from the cut-out's outline. It works on anything and is made automatically the first time you tick Relight.
  • Detailed shape (AI, people) works out the real shape of a person's body and clothes, for more convincing light on people.

Then:

  • Drag the sun on the round pad: its direction is where the light comes from; the centre means straight from the camera, the edge a low sun from the side.
  • Ground shadow follows this light (on by default): dragging the sun also turns the shadow on the ground away from it, and makes it longer for a low sun — one sun for the model and its shadow.
  • Brightness — strength of the light. Fill light — light on the side facing away (0 = black shadows); lower it for stronger, more directional light. Rim light — a bright edge from behind. Amount — blend between your original lighting (0) and the new light (1).
  • Skin softness — how softly light wraps around skin. Light colour and Rim colour.

Relight only changes shading; it never repaints your picture.

Several objects

Each object needs its own shadow. See Several objects in one picture.

Camera & lens look

Step 6 Camera & lens look makes a layer look shot with the same camera as the background: the same grain, softness, motion blur, haze and glow. A layer that is cleaner or sharper than its surroundings stands out even when its colours are right.

Copy the camera's blur & grain

Measures the background around the layer — its noise, softness and any motion blur — and copies them onto the layer (no AI). On a blurry, moving shot you get the same motion blur.

One-click fit copies only the grain. Use this button when you also want the softness and motion blur.

Lens: depth of field, bloom, colour fringing

Mostly for backgrounds that look too clean, like game screenshots and 3D renders. These live on the background layer's inspector too.

  • Depth of field: blurs what's far from the focus, like a real lens. It needs a depth map — click Measure depth (AI). Then Pick focus and click what should be sharp. Blur amount, Focus distance and Sharp range fine-tune it.
  • Bloom: glow around bright lights. Bloom size is how far it spreads, Bloom from how bright something must be to glow.
  • Colour fringing: red/blue edges like a cheap lens (chromatic aberration).
Less is more. Depth of field and bloom are easy to overdo; leave them off unless the background really needs them.

Game look

The Game look section (under Extras) makes a real photo look like low-resolution game graphics, for when the background is an old game and you look too detailed in it.

SettingWhat it does
Pixel sizebigger, blockier pixels (0 = off)
Colour stepsfewer colour levels, like old games (posterize; 0 = off)

Try a small pixel size (2–4) on a layer placed into a low-resolution game screenshot, so your detail matches the game's textures.

Working with video

PhotoHop edits photos and videos with the same tools. A still is simply a one-frame clip.

Importing video

Import a video like a photo. The first video into an empty project sets the canvas size, frame rate and length. Supported: MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM, AVI, M4V, including 10-bit and HDR phone video (HDR10/PQ, HLG, BT.2020), and image sequences.

Playing and scrubbing

  • Space plays and pauses. ←/→ step one frame. Home goes to the start.
  • Click or drag in the timeline ruler to move the playhead.
  • View > Video preview quality: *Half* (default) or *Quarter* for smooth playback on slower computers; *Full* for checking detail. Export always uses full quality.

Cutting yourself out of a video

On a video layer, 1 Cut out shows Remove background (AI, whole video): AI follows you through every frame (BiRefNet + MatAnyone 2). It takes a while — about 1–2 minutes for a 17-second 4K clip on a good graphics card, much longer on a laptop.

For a few problem frames, add Pen shapes and key them (◆) at those frames.

Tracking a region

AI > Track a region… — drag a box around something (a window, a sign) on the selected video layer. AI follows it through the clip and makes a moving mask. Another layer can then follow it: select that layer and use Follow the tracked region of in Timing & animation.

Timing & animation

The Timing & animation section and the timeline control when a layer appears and how its settings change over time.

Timing

  • Starts at — the time on the timeline where the layer begins.
  • Skip first — start a video this many seconds in (trim the beginning).

The canvas Duration (inspector with nothing selected) sets how long the whole project is.

Keyframes

A keyframe stores a value at a moment in time; between keyframes PhotoHop moves smoothly from one value to the next.

  • The ◆ next to a value sets or removes a keyframe at the playhead. Bright yellow = keyframe here; dim gold = animated, keyframe elsewhere; grey = not animated.
  • K toggles keyframes for position, size, rotation and opacity of the selected layer at once. The ◆ button in the timeline does the same.
  • Animate: every change adds a keyframe (auto-key) — when on, every change you make is keyed at the playhead. When off, a value that isn't animated yet changes for the whole clip; a value that already has keyframes gets a keyframe at the playhead.

Follow the tracked region of…

If another layer has an AI-tracked mask (from Track a region or a video cut-out), a button for it appears here. Click it to keyframe the selected layer so it moves with that tracked region — for example a screen replacement following a phone.

The timeline

Each layer is a bar. Diamonds on a bar are its keyframes. Drag in the ruler to scrub.

Replace a window or screen

Show another picture or clip through a window, TV or phone screen in your shot — for example a city at night outside your bedroom window. Reflections on the glass and anything in front of it (you, your arm, a laptop) stay.

Open it from the Replace a window or screen section (under Extras) or AI > Replace a window or screen…, with the layer that contains the window selected. A panel opens with four steps.

1. Glass

Mark the glass panes:

  • Type what to look for ("window pane", "tv screen") and click Find with AI — each pane becomes an editable shape.
  • Or Draw a pane with the pen. Shapes work like Pen shapes: set one to Subtract to keep something in front of the glass (on a photo).

2. What you see through it

  • Choose clip or image… picks the new view. It's fitted to the glass automatically and loops if it's shorter than your clip.
  • Fit to glass scales and centres it to cover every pane; Move view and the position/size controls adjust it; Loop view repeats a short clip.
  • Follow the camera — for a handheld shot: tracks the window frame and wall through the clip and keyframes the panes and the view so they stay locked to the window. Draw the panes on the current frame first.

3. Glass and camera look

  • Match the camera measures how soft and noisy your shot is and makes the view the same, so it doesn't look too clean.
  • Reflections — how much of the original glass (reflections, dirt) stays on top.
  • Out of focus, Haze / dirt, Glass tint, Brightness — make the view sit behind real glass.

4. Keep in front

Anything between the camera and the glass — you, your arms, a laptop — must stay in front of the new view. On video, click Draw a box around it: AI follows it through the clip. Dark sleeves against a night window are easy for the glass finder to miss, so box yourself too. On a photo, add a pane shape set to Subtract around it.

Turn off disables window replacement but keeps the settings.

AI tools

AI in PhotoHop is optional and has strict limits: it makes masks (which pixels belong to something) and settings (colour and light values you can see and edit). It never repaints your picture, except Remove an object, which paints only inside that object on a separate patch layer.

Setting up the AI tools

The AI tools are a separate Python package in the ai folder of PhotoHop (included in the download).

  1. Install uv (a Python installer).
  2. In the ai folder run: uv venv then uv pip install -e .
  3. For an NVIDIA graphics card, first install the CUDA build of PyTorch: uv pip install torch torchvision --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu128
  4. In PhotoHop, open AI > AI settings… and enter the path of that environment's Python (for example ai/.venv/bin/python, or ai\.venv\Scripts\python.exe on Windows). You can also set the PHOTOHOP_AI_PYTHON environment variable.

Models download automatically the first time each tool runs. Everything works on a CPU, just slower (about 40 seconds for a photo cut-out on a laptop). Results are saved with the project, so a slower computer can keep editing without re-running AI.

Blender round trip

For things PhotoHop can't fake in 2D — a 3D prop in your hand, shadows on uneven ground, reflections — send your shot to Blender, add 3D objects there, render, and bring the render back.

What you need

  • Blender 5.1 or newer.
  • The PhotoHop Bridge add-on: photohop_bridge.zip from the PhotoHop releases page. In Blender: *Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > Install from disk*.

1. Export to Blender

Select your layer (you) and choose File > Export to Blender…. Pick an empty folder. PhotoHop writes a *bundle*: your cut-out plate, the camera, the light from the Relight pad and, if you made them, the camera solve and body motion.

For moving shots, first run AI > Work out the camera movement so the Blender camera follows the phone's real movement. For props in your hands, run AI > Capture body motion (after removing the background of the clip).

2. In Blender

Load the bundle with the PhotoHop Bridge add-on. It builds the scene:

  • a camera matching your shot,
  • you as a card that casts shadows and hides props behind you,
  • a shadow-catcher floor at your feet,
  • a sun matching the Relight pad,
  • with body motion: your animated 3D body, plus PhotoHop right hand / PhotoHop left hand empties — parent a prop to one and it follows your hand.

Add your props, then use the add-on's Render for PhotoHop.

3. Import the render

File > Import Blender render… and pick the bundle folder. The renders come back as layers (props, shadows) that you can colour-match and adjust like anything else.

Projects

Troubleshooting

The colour match makes my layer look strange

  • Is the layer above the background in the Layers list? The match looks at what's below.
  • Leftover background in the cut-out (studio grey, green spill) is measured as part of you. Clean the cut-out first.
  • Several objects in one layer standing in different light? Split them first.
  • Lower Match strength, or try Match with AI instead (or the other way round).
  • Strongly coloured objects keep their colour on purpose; only the light, levels and saturation change. See Fit into the scene.

The shadow is in the wrong place

  • Click Find feet. If the cut-out includes floor or a reflection under the feet, trim it first.
  • Drag the orange dot with the Move tool to aim it.
  • On a slope, shorten the Length.

A layer's name is red

Its file could not be loaded — usually it was moved or renamed. Hover the name to see the path. Put the file back, or re-import it.

AI says it can't start

Open AI > AI settings… and check the Python path points at the environment where you installed the AI tools. See AI tools. Without an NVIDIA card everything still works, just slower.

Video playback is slow

View > Video preview quality > Quarter. Exports always use full quality.

Everything looks too sharp / too clean

Use Copy the camera's blur & grain in Camera & lens look. Game screenshots may also need a little Haze on distant layers.

I lost my work

PhotoHop doesn't auto-save. Save often with Ctrl+S. Undo (Ctrl+Z) goes back through every change in the session.

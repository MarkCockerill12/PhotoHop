# Your first composite

This walk-through puts a photo of a person into a game screenshot. It takes about five minutes. You need two pictures: a **background** (for example a screenshot from noclip.website) and a **photo of yourself** (a phone photo is fine).

## 1. Bring in the background

Drag the background picture onto PhotoHop's window, or use **File > Import photo / video (Ctrl+I)**. The first picture you import sets the size of the project (the **canvas**).

## 2. Bring in yourself

Import your photo the same way. It appears as a new layer on top. Select it in the Layers list (it's probably selected already).

## 3. Cut yourself out

Open step **1 Cut out** in the inspector and click **Remove background (AI)**. After a few seconds (up to a minute on a laptop without a graphics card) the background of your photo disappears.

Fix any mistakes: tick **Paint on the cut-out**, then use the **Brush (B)** to hide bits that should be gone and the **Eraser (E)** to bring back bits that went missing. See [Cut out](cutout).

> No AI installed? Use the **Pen (P)** to draw around yourself, or the **Select (M)** tool's lasso. See [Tools](tools).

## 4. Place and size yourself

Press **V** for the Move tool. Drag yourself to where you should stand; drag a **corner handle** to resize (hold **Shift** to stretch freely) and the **top handle** to rotate. Think about where your feet touch the ground and how tall you'd be at that distance — compare with doors, people or objects in the background. See [Position & size](position).

## 5. Fit into the scene

Open step **3 Fit into the scene** and click **✨ One-click fit**. PhotoHop:

- matches your colours to the scene around you (light colour, black level, saturation),
- turns on a **shadow** on the ground, starting at your feet,
- copies the background's film **grain** so you don't look too clean.

Drag **Match strength** to compare the original with the matched look. See [Fit into the scene](fit).

## 6. Fine-tune

- Shadow pointing the wrong way? With the Move tool, drag the **orange dot** to where the shadow of your head should land — copy the direction of shadows already in the scene. See [Shadow & light](shadow).
- Too bright, too dark, too orange? Open **4 Colour adjustments** and nudge Exposure or Temperature. See [Colour adjustments](colour).

## 7. Export

**File > Export…**, choose *PNG* or *JPEG* under **Current frame as image** and click **Export image…**. Save the project too (**Ctrl+S**) so you can come back to it. See [Saving & exporting](export).
